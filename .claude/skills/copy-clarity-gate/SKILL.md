**Copy Clarity Gate**

This skill is one gate for one recurring defect. Across many Lumenis copy projects the same correction keeps coming back in different costumes: "this" with nothing to point to, "yours" with no referent, "others" too loose, "this work" in an opening clause. Those are not separate problems. They are one problem: **the copy makes the reader do work the copy should have done.**

That defect is invisible to the faculty copywriting runs on, which is fluency. A vague referent or a clever header *sounds* like insider shorthand, and the smoothness is what hides the gap. So any check shaped like "does this read well?" passes it every time, because reading well is the camouflage. The only thing that catches it is a discrete, mechanical scan run as a separate last step, not woven into drafting.

**Flagging is not fixing.** Noticing that a word is vague and writing a note about it, then leaving the word in place, is the most common way this defect survives. The gate must resolve or rewrite, never just annotate.

**When to run it**

- Before delivering ANY copy deliverable, every time, including revisions and one-line tweaks.

- Immediately when Ted says "run the pass," "referent check," or "final pass": produce the full audit on whatever copy is currently on the table.

- After a format skill has produced the copy. This gate is the last step, downstream of `lumenis-brand-voice`, `lumenis-kol-webinar-slides`, and `lumenis-sales-hype-slides`.

This gate does not own compliance (prohibited terms, disclaimers, outcome-claim rules). Those stay with the format skills and with Ted's prohibited-terms list. This gate owns clarity only.

**The core rule**

**The reader should never have to do work the copy should have done.** That work shows up in exactly three forms. Run all three checks, in order, as a deliberate scan.

**1. Decode — does each header say plainly what the thing is?**

Every header, slide title, subject line, and hook must state the point outright, with no clever phrase the reader has to unpack first. If the reader has to infer what it means, rewrite it plainly.

- Fails: a curiosity-gap or aphoristic title that assumes the reader already gets the point.

- Passes: the reader's situation in plain words, then the thing named outright.

**2. Trace — does each "why it matters" land on a concrete reader effect?**

Follow every value statement down the chain: what specifically changes, for whom, and why do they care? Stop at "improves outcomes" or "great for engagement" and it fails, because those are abstractions the reader still has to cash out. Trace it all the way to the concrete effect on that specific reader.

**3. Locate — can the reader point to what every referent refers to, using only earlier words?**

This is the mechanical one, and the one that slips through most. Run it as a literal list, not a vibe:

1. List every **demonstrative** ("this / that / these / those"), every **pronoun** ("it / them / they / yours"), and every **definite noun phrase** ("the surgeons / the work / the space / the number").

2. Next to each, name the **exact earlier word or phrase** it points to.

3. If a referent cannot be resolved from text that appears *before* it, rewrite it plainly.

**Auto-fail rules — no need to deliberate, these fail on sight:**

- Anaphora ("this X," "that X") in an **opening clause, headline, eyebrow, or slide title.** Nothing precedes it, so there is nothing for it to point at.

- A definite noun phrase ("the surgeons," "the approach") introducing a group or thing that is never identified anywhere. It presents something specific and known when the reader cannot tell what it is.

- A pronoun whose nearest candidate antecedent is two or more nouns back, so the reader has to guess which one.

Fluent copy is the camouflage: a vague referent reads smooth, which is why a read-for-flow misses it and only this list catches it.

**Output convention (default is LOUD)**

- **Substantial deliverables** (decks, emails, campaign or public-facing copy, one-pagers): show the short three-part audit next to the copy so Ted can see the gate ran, not just trust it. Keep it tight:

  - **Decode:** one line confirming headers say what they are, or naming the fix.

  - **Trace:** one line confirming each stake is concrete, or naming the fix.

  - **Locate:** the referent list with each antecedent, or the flagged-and-rewritten items.

- **Quick one-line asks:** run all three silently and deliver clean copy only.

- **"Run the pass":** force the full audit on demand, regardless of size.

**Worked example (from a real correction)**

Draft line (opening clause):

The cataract and refractive surgeons pushing **this work** forward have shared how they catch the unhappy patient...

**Locate audit:** "this work" is a demonstrative in the opening clause. Nothing precedes it, so it has no antecedent. The reader has to read the whole sentence, infer the topic, then back-fill what "this work" meant. Auto-fail.

Rewrite (referent resolved by naming the thing outright):

Leading cataract and refractive surgeons have worked out how to catch the unhappy patient hiding behind a perfect 20/20 number, and they've put **their method** into two pieces you can use: a Clinical Field Guide with **their** approach to spotting **that patient**, and a Practice Implementation Kit with **their** plan for catching **that patient** routinely.

**Locate audit, clean version:**

- "their method" → the surgeons' way of catching the patient, named in the clause just before.

- "that patient" → the unhappy patient hiding behind a 20/20 number, named earlier.

- "their" (x2) → leading cataract and refractive surgeons.

All resolve backward. Passes.

**Why this is a gate and not a reminder**

A reminder ("keep clarity in mind") gets skipped silently under attention pressure, especially right after other edits, when focus has moved elsewhere. A gate forces an artifact: the referent list. A reminder can be waved through with a fluent read; a list cannot be produced without actually checking each item. That difference is the whole point. When in doubt, produce the list.
