# Jacob Rullo, MD, PhD

**Current affiliation:** Assistant Professor and Clinician Scientist, Departments of Ophthalmology and Biomedical and Molecular Sciences, Queen's University; ophthalmologist with hospital privileges at Kingston Health Sciences Centre, Kingston, ON, Canada

## Longer biography

Dr. Jacob Rullo is an ophthalmologist and clinician scientist at Queen's University in Kingston, Ontario, where he has been an Assistant Professor in the Departments of Ophthalmology and Biomedical and Molecular Sciences since July 2020. He sees patients at Kingston Health Sciences Centre and runs the Vision Science Research Laboratory at Queen's.

He earned a BHSc at McMaster University in 2006, then completed the MD/PhD program in Laboratory Medicine and Pathobiology at the University of Toronto (PhD 2012, MD 2015), where his research used live-cell imaging to study how white blood cells adhere under blood flow. From 2015 to 2020 he completed his ophthalmology residency at Queen's alongside a postdoctoral research fellowship, and he received Royal College of Physicians and Surgeons of Canada certification in ophthalmology in 2020.

His laboratory studies the eye's microenvironments: small molecules in the aqueous and vitreous that may serve as biomarkers or drug targets in age-related macular degeneration, glaucoma and retinal degeneration, and the immune biology and microbiome of the ocular surface. He has also studied immune responses to intravitreal anti-VEGF injections. His publications include first-author papers in *Scientific Reports*, the *Journal of Steroid Biochemistry and Molecular Biology* and the *Canadian Journal of Ophthalmology*, and recent case reports in neuro-ophthalmology.

## Short biography

Dr. Jacob Rullo is an ophthalmologist and Assistant Professor at Queen's University in Kingston, Ontario, and practises at Kingston Health Sciences Centre. He holds an MD and a PhD in Laboratory Medicine and Pathobiology from the University of Toronto, completed his ophthalmology residency at Queen's, and leads research on the eye's immune environment and the ocular surface.
