# OptiLIFT regulatory language and do's and don'ts

## Sources

- **Legal do's and don'ts:** "OptiLIFT legal - Dos and dont's" (undated, Word and PDF versions; the PDF adds reference footnotes).
- **Indication and warnings:** "IFU, Warnings and Risks - OptiLIFT"; OptiLIFT Quick Reference Guide, PB-00094762 Rev. A, p.8; OptiLIFT legal-language title cards, including testimonial cards PB-00096711 Rev A and PB-00096712 Rev A.

## Indication for use (verbatim)

> The OptiLIFT System and its accessories are intended for dermatological procedures requiring ablation and resurfacing of the skin when using VoluDerm Energy (Microneedling Applicator). It is also intended for use in dermatologic and general surgical procedures for non-invasive treatment when using RF Energy and for muscle conditioning to stimulate healthy muscles (OptiLIFT Applicator). OptiLIFT System is not intended to be used in conjunction with therapy or treatment of medical diseases or medical conditions of any kind. OptiLIFT System is intended to be operated by a trained professional who is present to monitor treatment.

## Warnings and risks (verbatim)

> OptiLIFT System is contraindicated for patients with pacemakers, defibrillators, any implanted electronic device, or metal implanted in the treatment area. Side effects may appear either at the time of treatment or shortly after. Side effects may include any of the following: prolonged or significant pain, damage to natural skin texture (blister, burn), excessive skin redness (erythema), excessive swelling (edema), fragile skin bruising, excessive itching, change of pigmentation (hyper-pigmentation or hypopigmentation), scarring transient skin break-out such as acne and pimples. For a complete list of contraindications, please refer to the User Manual.

## What this means for copy

- **OptiLIFT is not a dry eye treatment.** The indication is muscle conditioning of healthy muscles, and the device "is not intended to be used in conjunction with therapy or treatment of medical diseases or medical conditions of any kind." Never say or imply that OptiLIFT treats, improves or relieves dry eye, MGD or their signs and symptoms.
- **Describe what it does:** dynamic muscle stimulation (DMSt) and RF technologies that stimulate the muscles in the periorbital area, tone and tighten the lower eyelid, enhance blinking, and address lower lid laxity and impaired blinking.
- **Study results only in study context.** "Improves TBUT" and "improves lower lid laxity and related blinking mechanics" are permitted only when speaking about the study, with the reference.
- **Location:** "applied on the skin in the peri-orbital area." Do not mention proximity to the eye or treatment on the eyelids.
- **Regulatory status:** "FDA-cleared technology," never "FDA-approved."
- **Dry eye statistics are about the condition, not the device.** Epidemiology from the [claims library](../claims-reference/optilift.md), such as lid-closure prevalence in dry eye patients, can describe patients. It must not be joined to OptiLIFT as a claimed outcome.

## Open questions for the regulatory owner

1. **"Treatment."** The don't list rules out "Treatment," yet the indication, warnings and quick reference guide use "treatment," "treatment area" and "sessions." The likely reading is that OptiLIFT must not be called a treatment *for a condition*, while procedural words are acceptable. Confirm.
2. **Eyelid wording.** "Tone and tighten the lower eyelid" is a do, but "treatment on eyelids" is a don't. Confirm that "lower eyelid" may name the outcome as long as the copy says the device is applied to the skin in the peri-orbital area.
3. **Operator wording.** The IFU document, the quick reference guide and testimonial cards say "trained professional"; the combined OptiLIGHT and OptiLIFT title card and brochure PB-00088980 Rev A say "licensed healthcare practitioner." Confirm which is current.
4. **Contraindications.** The quick reference guide FAQ also names insulin pumps and cochlear implants, which the warnings text does not list. Confirm whether patient materials should include them.
5. **"Clinically validated" footnote.** The legal PDF still cites "data on file; manuscript under preparation." Confirm the published Chelnis 2025 reference can replace it.
6. **Dry eye positioning.** Existing sales materials pair OptiLIFT with dry eye patients (for example, the Chelnis study's dry eye population and lid-closure epidemiology). Confirm how far dry eye context may appear in OptiLIFT pieces given the "not intended ... for medical diseases or medical conditions" sentence.
